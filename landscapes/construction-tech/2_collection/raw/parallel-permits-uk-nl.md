# Where Permit Software Wins in Britain and the Netherlands

## Executive Summary

- **Two Distinct Buying Layers**: An applicant-facing submission service is not the system in which an authority manages and decides a case. England's Planning Portal and council back-office systems, and the Dutch Omgevingsloket and municipal VTH systems, should be mapped separately. [1][9][10] -> Compare suppliers by workflow layer, not by a single market-share figure.
- **Idox's Defensible Position**: Idox reported **GBP 89.8m group revenue** in FY2025, but did not break out planning-software revenue in the evidence reviewed. Blackburn with Darwen selected it after **three responses** to a tender sent to **29 suppliers**. [7][12] -> Treat the group figure as scale evidence, not the size of the planning market.
- **A Real Incumbent-Switching Window**: Blackburn awarded a replacement planning, land-charges and building-control system for **five years**, extendable by up to **five more**; Epsom and Ewell separately contracted for Idox maintenance through **October 2028**. [12][1] -> Track replacement tenders and renewal dates, not just new council budgets.
- **Publicly Funded Competition**: The government reported **43** authorities receiving **GBP 100,000 each** under one Digital Planning Improvement Fund round; by March 2026, PlanX was used by **16** authorities and **23** more had one-year adoption funding. [18][15] -> Test whether new front ends actually displace incumbent case-management contracts.
- **Dutch Rules Are a Separate Product Market**: Dutch authorities deliver applicable rules to the national register used by the Omgevingsloket, while commercial vendors sell plan authoring and VTH case handling. [5][11][10] -> Specify which data, rules and decisions a procurement must cover.
- **Automation Is Not Approval**: Struck markets location-specific regulatory answers and a permit-free check; UK Planning Gateway markets application preparation and validation support. These are vendor claims, not proof of legally binding decisions or measured error rates. [19][14] -> Require demonstrations against actual local rules and independent accuracy testing.
- **Commercial Visibility Is Uneven**: Listed Idox publishes group accounts; most private vendors do not publish product revenue, authority-specific customer counts, sales-cycle statistics or comparable pricing. [7][12] -> Mark those fields undisclosed rather than extrapolating.

**Scope.** The UK evidence below principally describes **England**: planning law, digital-planning grants and building-control arrangements are not uniform across the four UK nations. The Dutch evidence includes national infrastructure and products sold to municipalities and other competent authorities. **Evidence labels:** **P** = government publication, public tender, annual report or other primary record; **V** = vendor's own description, useful for product identification but weak for unverified performance or market-share claims; **S** = a single secondary-source claim.

## England's Three Layers: Submission, Council Decisions and Building Control

Planning Portal, council planning casework and building control answer different questions. A professional may use a national submission route, but the local planning authority must validate, consult, determine and publish its case; the software purchased for those tasks is a separate contract. Arcus describes planning-application, enforcement, appeal and consultation workflows, while a council tender identifies Idox Uniform as supporting planning and regulatory functions. These product descriptions establish function, not adoption share. [20][1]

| Layer and product | Buyer or payer | Work actually performed | Evidence and limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Planning Portal**, operated by **TerraQuest** | Applicants pay applicable submission/service charges; authorities receive applications | Applicant-facing national route for preparing and submitting planning applications | Operator identification is independently reported; the exact current fee was not established here. **S** [21] |
| **Council development-management systems**: Idox, Arcus and alternatives | Councils or shared planning services | Intake, validation, consultations, case management, registers and decisions | Specific Idox contracts are documented; Arcus's feature inventory is **V**. [12][20] |
| **Building-control systems**: council systems and specialist approver software | Council building-control teams or private registered building control approvers (RBCAs) | Plans, inspections and building-control case records | Barnbrook describes an approver-specific AIP system; its performance claims are **V**. [13] |
| **Higher-risk-building approval** | Applicants deal with the **Building Safety Regulator** | The regulator is the building-control authority for covered higher-risk work | This is not an ordinary private-approver route. **P** [6] |

**Decision implication:** A tool that makes submission easier can win users without winning a council's back-office tender. Conversely, a back-office supplier needs a credible connection to applicant intake and statutory records.

## Idox, Arcus and Procurement: What Two Council Decisions Actually Show

**Idox** (idoxgroup.com) is a publicly traded UK software group headquartered in Theale; an external company profile reports **645 employees**, but its workforce count and founding-year treatment were not corroborated against the annual report in this review and should be treated as indicative. Its FY2025 **GBP 89.8m** is *total group revenue*, not planning or building-control revenue; reported recurring revenue was **GBP 59.734m**, approximately **66.5%** of group revenue. [7] Idox's relevant offer spans planning, building control and related public-protection casework; its assertion that it is the UK's leading government-planning supplier is **V**, not an independently measured share. [22][23]

**Blackburn with Darwen is the competitive-award case.** A council decision dated **18 October 2024** names Idox Software Ltd as the preferred replacement for planning, land charges and building control. Procurement was approved in **July 2024**; **29** suppliers were invited, **three** responded, and Idox's evaluation score was **83.8**. The award is **five years plus extensions of up to five years**. The council disclosed **GBP 102,000** of capital implementation costs, but the report did **not** give a total contract value; it said annual running costs would be **GBP 9,000 below** the department's existing budget. [12] Thus there is evidence of a contestable opportunity and a migration cost, but not a defensible average software price.

**Epsom and Ewell illustrates the opposite route.** A published contract for **GBP 276,340 excluding VAT** covers Idox software and maintenance from **22 November 2025 to 29 October 2028**. Its notice cites single-supplier technical reasons and refers to the Uniform system's essential regulatory functions. [1] Taken together, the cases show both replacement competition and incumbent continuity. They do **not** show that all councils buy without competition or that every contract lasts ten years.

## PlanX and Applicant Startups Challenge the Front Door First

The **MHCLG Digital Planning Programme** funds authorities and shared digital infrastructure rather than acting as another conventional software reseller. A 2024 government announcement identifies **43** planning authorities each receiving **GBP 100,000** for digital-planning improvement and **37** PropTech pilot projects involving **49** authorities; these are distinct measures, not 80 additional software customers. Another round provided **46** authorities with **GBP 50,000** each. [18][17] Funded work includes datasets, digital capabilities, development-management improvements, land assessment and citizen engagement, not solely automated permit decisions. [18][17]

**PlanX** (opendigitalplanning.org/planx) lets authorities jointly build data-driven planning services through a low-code editor. The March 2026 government account reports **16** authorities using it, **56** services created, and **23** additional authorities funded to adopt it for **one year**; those adopters must bring at least one public service live within **12 months**. A separate offer of **GBP 20,000** concerned targeted back-office improvements, not PlanX's price. [3][15] BOPS and related open digital-planning work belong in the council-back-office pilot discussion, but the evidence gathered does not establish that they have displaced Idox at national scale. The strategic test is live transaction volume, integration and durable funding after the grant, not the number of pilot awards.

## The Dutch DSO Divides the Omgevingswet Market Into Rules, Plans and VTH

The **Omgevingsloket**, part of the Dutch digital system **DSO**, offers an address/activity-based **Vergunningcheck**: an initiator answers questions to check whether an idea is permissible or requires an application. Government bodies themselves supply the *toepasbare regels* (usable question-and-answer rules) for the national **Registratie toepasbare regels**. These are distinct from legal plan text and from the authority's subsequent permit decision. **P** [9][5]

A municipality, province or water board may therefore buy **plansoftware** to author and publish its environmental rules, software to convert those rules into usable checks, and **VTH** software for *vergunningverlening, toezicht en handhaving* (permitting, inspection and enforcement). The national portal is shared infrastructure; it does not make every local case-management purchase disappear. Centric explicitly positions its VTH product for municipalities, environmental agencies and ministries; vendor product descriptions alone do not establish installed share among provinces and water boards. [10]

## Dutch Purchasing, Switching and the Limits of an AI Precheck

A Dutch TenderNed notice for **Amersfoort** concerns supply, implementation, hosting and maintenance of a VTH application. That bundle demonstrates that authorities purchase more than a seat licence; the particular award price, term and winning bidder were not established from the material reviewed. **P** [8] A secondary account describes a **six-year** VTH SaaS contract awarded to Visma Roxit for **Midden-Drenthe**; treat its term and award as **weak until checked against the underlying tender**. **S** [30] This is evidence of multi-year buying, not evidence that every Dutch municipality, province or water board follows one contract length.

**Struck is a useful boundary case.** Its public site says it can bring together Dutch municipal, regional and national rules for a specific plot and describes an AI assistant, permit-free check and regulation library. Customer testimonials claim substantial time savings, including a municipality's account of faster source-backed answers; these are **marketing testimonials**, not a controlled accuracy or productivity study. [19] An architect may pay for faster early-stage design checks and a municipality may procure a citizen or caseworker interface, but neither use turns the precheck into the legal decision by the competent authority. The appropriate evaluation is a sample of real parcels, current rules, citations, edge cases and escalation to a qualified reviewer.

**Commercial model and sales cycle.** Authority-side licences or SaaS, hosting, integration, migration, implementation and support are paid by the procuring public body or an inter-authority service; applicant-side research and workflow subscriptions are generally paid by professional users, while submission charges fall to the applicant or agent. Blackburn's approval-to-award sequence from **July to October 2024** alone took roughly **three months**, and the resulting contract can run **five to ten years**; that is a case study, **not** a measured median sales cycle. [12] Pilot-funded adoption has its own timetable: PlanX's newly funded authorities have **12 months** to put a public service live. [15] No credible cross-vendor average sales cycle was disclosed in the evidence reviewed.

## Synthesis: The Contest Is Over Interfaces, Rules and Renewal Dates

| Dimension | UK incumbent authority suites | UK public digital tools and applicant products | Dutch plans, VTH and prechecks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Maintain statutory casework and connected records | Improve the submission front door, data availability or early feasibility | Publish legal plans and usable rules, handle VTH cases, or explain compliance |
| **Buyer and horizon** | Council; competitive replacement or multi-year maintenance | Government-funded authority adoption, or recurring professional spend | Competent authority for plan/VTH systems; practitioner or authority for prechecks |
| **Main advantage** | Embedded processes and integration | Reusable services and less friction before validation | Alignment with national DSO interfaces and local rules |
| **Main constraint** | Expensive migration and potentially closed incumbent integration | Pilots and user counts do not prove back-office replacement | An AI answer does not constitute an authoritative permit decision |
| **Best diligence question** | When does the contract expire, and what must migrate? | What percentage of submissions reach a valid case without rework? | Which party maintains legal text, usable rules and decision records? |

The non-obvious divergence is that **PlanX competes first for how an application is assembled**, while an Idox renewal concerns who runs the statutory case. In the Netherlands, **Struck's answer layer** can similarly sit above a DSO check and a municipal VTH system rather than replacing either. [3][1][19][10] The practical strategy for a supplier or investor is to map interfaces and expiries authority by authority, test automation on real applications, and avoid equating vendor-wide revenue, marketing customer counts or grant recipients with permit-software market share.
