# Spain Construction Technology: A Vendor Map for Buyers

## Executive Summary

- **Price Data Shapes Procurement**: Presto, CYPE and ITeC offer different combinations of estimates, measurement, price information and project controls [2][3][4][5] -> Check which database, file exchange and cost-control process a tender actually requires.
- **Ownership Crosses Borders**: Schneider Electric completed its takeover of RIB Software [6]; Cegid markets both Menfis and Sigrid [7][8] -> Distinguish a Spanish product team from its international parent.
- **ERP Is Not BIM**: Sigrid is marketed for contractor-wide cost and departmental control, while BIM tools serve shared models and design information [8][9] -> Budget for integration rather than treating either as an all-in-one solution.
- **Permitting Has Separate Operators**: Madrid lists EQA as an urban-licence collaborating entity; its separate MADRIDdbp procurement had an estimated value of **EUR9,326,691.30**, excluding VAT [10][11] -> Do not confuse a regulated intermediary with the municipal software contractor.
- **Digital Permit Claims Need Qualification**: Madrid described MADRIDdbp as a future municipal platform when it approved the contract in **June 2025**; CHEK reported its final project event in **September 2025** [12][13] -> Request evidence of production deployment and the actual software supplier for each municipality.
- **Monitoring Funding Is Not Market Share**: The available company records show about **$5.46M** disclosed funding for SAALG and **$1.29M** for CheckToBuild, but no independently measured Spain-wide adoption for either [1] -> Test project-level performance before scaling.
- **Disclosure Is Uneven**: Some records provide headcounts but no vendor-specific revenue or Spanish user totals [1] -> Read 'not disclosed' literally, not as zero.

**Reading the map.** P = municipal, registry or other official primary source; C = company or project-party statement, reliable for what the party says but product reach is **marketing, single-source/weak**; D = third-party company-data estimate, not an audited filing. **ND** means no adequately supported public number in the material reviewed. Employee counts are company-record figures, not measured Spanish product-user counts. Lifecycle shorthand: F = site selection/feasibility; L = land acquisition/financing; S = surveys; Dsg = design; Pmt = permits/licences; T = tendering/estimating; Cn = construction management/monitoring; H = handover; O = operations; R = renovation.

## Presto, Arquímedes and BEDEC: Different Kinds of Cost Evidence

RIB calls Presto an integrated BIM-oriented cost and time management program [2]. CYPE describes Cost Estimator as automated early building-budget estimation [3], while ITeC describes BEDEC as a construction-products database with prices, specifications, environmental information and BIM objects [4]. TCQ/TCQi covers budgeting, planning, work tracking and asset maintenance [5]. These are **not interchangeable**: a price database supplies reference inputs; a measurement/estimating program structures quantities and costs; a contractor ERP reconciles live delivery with the enterprise ledger.

**Case study: a contractor bidding a public project.** It can take a reference price from BEDEC, create quantities and estimates in TCQ, Presto or CYPE, then manage commitments and actual costs in an ERP. That workflow is an illustrative procurement architecture, **not a verified interoperability claim**. Ask each shortlisted vendor to demonstrate exchange of the actual bill-of-quantities format, revisions, regional price bases and audit history on the same real tender. ITeC's nonprofit status [16] contrasts with Presto's Schneider group ownership [6]: governance and continuity deserve separate tests from estimating features.

## Sigrid and BrickControl: Contractor Controls Beyond the Estimate

Cegid's Sigrid page promises to track job deviations and integrate company departments [8]; BrickControl markets cloud-based scheduling, budgets and construction-project management [20]. Those are **company descriptions, not independent implementation success rates**. A named contractor, Construcciones San Martín, discusses its Sigrid implementation [19], providing a stronger indication of use than a generic product page but not a representative adoption survey.

**Case study: replacing spreadsheet-based job control.** The relevant choice is whether purchase orders, subcontracts, payroll, certificates and estimates reconcile to the same job-code structure. A firm can have attractive field dashboards and still fail that test. Trial two projects of different size, reconcile cost-to-complete with accounting, and require a data-export and migration plan. Product marketing and a single named implementation cannot establish sector-wide penetration.

## BIM, CDEs and Review: Check the Information Handoff

Autodesk has a Spanish-language BIM offering [22]; Construsoft says it distributes Trimble's Tekla tools in Spain [23]; Bimetica offers downloadable BIM objects [21]. These play different roles: authoring, model information management and product content. A common data environment (CDE) combines information from models and other sources, rather than serving as a substitute for design software [9]. Also screen the international platforms Dalux, PlanRadar, Oracle Aconex and Procore when relevant, but do **not** infer a measured Spanish user base from their international reputation.

**Case study: design review becoming handover.** A developer might specify a model, a shared issue register and structured product data, yet the operator still cannot use the result if identifiers or revisions are lost at handover. Request an open-format export and a demonstration that one design issue, site observation and asset record retain their links through H and O. Spain's official BIM procurement observatory tracks public tenders requiring BIM; it does not publish vendor market share [26].

## Madrid's ECU and DBP: Regulated Review Is Not a Software Award

Madrid says applicants may choose voluntarily between a collaborating ECU and direct municipal processing [27]. Its public listing identifies **EQA Licencias Urbanísticas S.L.** at a Madrid address [10]. EQA's own statement that it has operated as a Madrid ECU since 2010 is **C; single-source/weak for duration** [28]. An ECU helps process or check applications under the applicable system; it should not be described as the vendor building Madrid's digital permit platform. Other authorized ECUs should be checked against the municipality's current list, not inferred from a vendor directory.

**Case study: MADRIDdbp.** The city describes a proposed digital platform joined to an urban-planning rules engine and topographic information to automate checks [24]. It published tender **300/2025/00064** on **30 June 2025**, with estimated value **EUR9,326,691.30 excluding VAT** [11]. The announcement identifies a procurement, **not** a proved winning developer, delivered installation, or permit-processing outcome. Buyers should obtain the award notice, subcontractor details, production status and supported rule set before attributing the platform to any software house.

**Case study: Málaga and CHEK.** The EU-backed CHEK initiative describes a collaborative digital-building-permit toolkit with municipal and software-development partners, and reported a final event in September 2025 [13][25]. The accessible evidence does **not** establish who, if anyone, supplies a separately deployed Málaga permit system. Treat any Málaga pilot, toolkit component, and live municipal permit service as distinct until a city contract or project deliverable links them. This prevents mistakenly presenting a research consortium as a single commercial vendor.

## SAALG and CheckToBuild: Capital Versus Verifiable Site Reach

The company records describe **SAALG** as AI-supported project monitoring from Barcelona and **CheckToBuild** as building inspection from Málaga [1]. Their disclosed funding totals differ substantially, but neither record establishes how many active Spanish jobsites they cover. **011h** is a much larger recorded employer and capital recipient, yet its construction-delivery proposition should not be conflated with a reality-capture application [1]. Aridditive adds 3D concrete printing and traceability, a different physical automation bet [1].

**Case study: monitoring procurement.** An owner considering SAALG alongside site-inspection or capture tools should specify what each system measures, baseline survey accuracy, update frequency, who verifies exceptions and how observations reach the site manager. A robot prototype or funding round is not a substitute for demonstrated safe deployment and measured return on a Spanish project. For early startups, seek customer references and production evidence rather than extrapolating market share from fundraising.

## Synthesis: Buy by Handoff, Not by Vendor Category

| Decision dimension | Cost stack | Enterprise/BIM stack | Permit stack | Site-tech startups |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Quantities, price bases and estimate revisions | ERP controls commitments; BIM/CDE preserves models and issues | ECU performs regulated intermediary work; city platform automates checks | Sensors, capture, analytics or physical automation |
| Scope and buyer | Estimators, design offices, contractors | Contractors, designers, developers, operators | Municipalities and permit applicants | Site managers, contractors, owners |
| Main failure mode | Reference prices mistaken for contracted cost | Model and financial records diverge | Pilot mistaken for a live city service or intermediary mistaken for software supplier | Fundraising mistaken for deployments |
| Evidence available | Spanish vendor product pages and ITeC institution [2][16] | Product descriptions and one named ERP case [8][19] | Municipal listings and procurement; consortium account [10][11][25] | Dated company-data funding records, not Spanish reach [1] |

**Decision:** Build the shortlist around the information handoffs from **F/L/S to Dsg/Pmt/T, Cn, H, O and R**, not around one fashionable label. This map has the strongest primary evidence for the legal status of ITeC, Madrid's ECU listing and the city's tender; descriptions of product performance and distribution are mostly self-reported. No source here supports a vendor-by-vendor Spanish market-share ranking or undisclosed revenue. Require a local customer reference, transparent legal counterparty, data-export test, price-base compatibility and production permit status before committing.
