# Correcting a 24-Company Construction-Technology Registry

## Executive Summary

- **Acquisition Is Not Funding**: Nemetschek acquired Bluebeam in **2014**, Hilti acquired Fieldwire in **2021**, and Thoma Bravo acquired Foundation Software in **2020**. Record these as ownership events, not venture rounds. [1][2][3]
- **Corporate Identity Matters**: CoreLogic became Cotality in **2025**; Dodge Data & Analytics merged with The Blue Book in **2021**. Their predecessor histories should not automatically become founding dates for the current brands. [4][5]
- **Investor Checks Correct Capital Claims**: ALICE's announced **$47M Series B value includes a term loan**, while Entrata called its **$200M** Blackstone transaction a *minority investment*, not a Series D. Preserve those distinctions. [6][7]
- **Products Need Narrower Labels**: ArchiLabs sells broader architectural CAD/BIM automation, Fresco specializes in **Division 8**, and Adaptive describes itself as project accounting, not simply job costing. [8][9][10]
- **Robots Are Not Pure Software**: Built Robotics markets a physical robotic pile driver, and Dusty Robotics markets a physical layout-printing robot. Classify them as robotics vendors rather than pure-play software vendors. [11][12]
- **Aggregator Figures Remain Unverified**: Most exact employee counts, ARR estimates and cumulative funding totals in the registry lack supporting primary-source disclosures in the material examined. Do not promote them to verified company facts.
- **Different End Markets**: Entrata sells multifamily property-management software, Dealpath addresses real-estate investment workflows, and 1001 describes AI for physical infrastructure. None is accurately described solely as a construction-project-management vendor. [13][14][15]

**Reading the tables:** A date of **undated** means the cited primary page did not establish a publication date in the material available. **Unconfirmed** means *no supporting primary source was found*, not that the registry figure is false. A dash in the source column means there is no source supporting that particular field. Where a financing announcement does not establish that it remains the *latest* transaction, the table verifies the announced event only. For companies without an explicit ownership entry in the input, the ownership row uses “not supplied.”

## Synthesis - which registry errors change the company description?

| Company or group | Material registry correction | Practical classification |
|---|---|---|
| **Cotality; Dodge Construction Network** | Rebrand or merger changes the relevant entity history. [4][5] | Property-data successor; merged construction-data/network business, respectively. |
| **Bluebeam; Fieldwire; Foundation Software** | Acquisition consideration or a PE buyout is not a venture funding round. [1][54][3] | Operating software subsidiaries/portfolio companies, not independent fundraising startups. |
| **Built Robotics; Dusty Robotics** | Actual offering includes an autonomous physical machine. [11][12] | Robotics/hardware-and-software, not pure SaaS. |
| **ArchiLabs; Fresco; Adaptive; Dealpath; Entrata** | Houston/data-center-only, generic takeoff, job-costing-only, construction-investment and construction-management labels respectively miss the supported product scope. [21][8][9][10][14][13] | Architectural CAD, Division 8 estimating, project accounting, real-estate investment software and multifamily property-management software. |
| **1001 AI; Cupix; Digs** | Construction-only AI, progress-monitoring-only and home-passport-only descriptions omit central functions or broader markets. [15][40][44] | Infrastructure AI, spatial digital twins and home-builder collaboration, respectively. |

The most consequential distinction is **what a transaction changes**. An investor taking a minority position, an acquirer buying a business, and a predecessor changing its name affect different registry fields; none should automatically populate “total raised.” The second distinction is **what is actually sold**: an actively marketed robot, specialist estimating product or property-data service can belong in a broader built-world landscape without being classified as general construction SaaS.
